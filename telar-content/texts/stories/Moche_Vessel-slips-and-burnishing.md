---
title: "Slips and burnishing"
---

The slips were likely made by thinning clay with water to improve workability and ease of application. The red slip is a distinct color from the vessel, which suggests the use of separation techniques to increase the Fe₂O₃ content within the slip. The white slip was likely made from a lighter clay with lower iron content. PIXE analysis of other Moche stirrup-spout vessels found that white slips were more abundant in calcium and less abundant in iron than red areas, which helps explain the lighter color. The slips are allowed to dry, and the entire vessel was possibly burnished to achieve the smooth and slightly reflective appearance.
