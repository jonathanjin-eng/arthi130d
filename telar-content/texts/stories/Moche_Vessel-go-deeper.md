---
title: "Go Deeper"
---

The vessel is primarily made of low-temperature fired terracotta with white and red slips applied to the exterior. The chip at the rim of the stirrup spout provides insights into the composition of the clay. The light orange or rusty-red appearance suggests the use of Fe2O3\-containing clay fired at a lower temperature. Clays fired at a higher temperature exhibit a darker and duller red color due to the clay increasing in density and vitrification (becoming glass-like). There appear to be multiple layers of slip applied on the vessel (a red base slip with white line designs). The slips were likely applied by suspending clay in water and painting a thin layer on the vessel prior to firing. The red slip is a distinct color from the matrix, which suggests the use of separation techniques to increase the Fe2O3 content within the slip. Using PIXE spectrometry, other Moche vessels have been analyzed, and their white slips were found to be more abundant in CaO and less abundant in Fe2O3, which helps explain the white color of the slip. Lastly, the dark markings and patches near the underside of the vessel are characteristic of fire clouding. Fire clouding happens when the ceramic comes into contact with "another vessel, a chunk of unburned fuel, or the floor of the kiln" during firing, creating a local low-oxygen environment, which leads to the incomplete oxidation of Fe2O3. Contact with intense smoke and unburnt fuel can deposit carbon onto the clay, further contributing to the dark discoloration. The black, wavy striped markings, however, seem to be intentional and part of a design that has worn off with time. This may be the result of post-firing organic black pigment painting. This technique involved painting liquids from plants onto the vessel and carefully heating it over a fire, allowing the organic material to carbonize. These markings are likely less durable than slips, which accounts for their faded appearance.
