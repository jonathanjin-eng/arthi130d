As is with many works made during this time period, if it was portable such as a stirrup-spout vessel, it was at risk of looting and recontextualization. Because of this, it can be difficult to determine who exactly had these objects because oftentimes archaeologists cannot themselves be the ones to excavate the objects. However, due to the ability to 'mass produce' these objects by making them with a mold, it is more likely that a high number of them were made, making them relatively accessible. Additionally, there are accounts of simple pots such as this vessel being plentiful in communities for purposes of cooking and storage. Works such as the portrait vessels, however, would have necessitated more specialization to create and the portraits were most likely high status individuals. Overall, simple ceramic works would most likely be available to the general public while more specialized works were reserved for an elite population.
